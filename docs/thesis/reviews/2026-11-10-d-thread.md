## Weakens

### T1. The first half of RQ1 is answered by a design, and three of its four parts have no evidence behind them

- **Where:** p. 4, `01-introduction.tex:185-187` (RQ1); p. 30, `04-content.tex:493-505` (Answer, first paragraph); p. 55, `07-conclusion.tex:22-33`.
- **Quoted:** RQ1: "How can heterogeneous lecture PDFs be reliably transformed into structured, grounded educational content …". Answer: "The answer this chapter gives has four parts. Identity by content … Decide before generating … Route by the page … Write as you go".
- **What is wrong:**
  - **"Reliably" and "grounded" are now conceded as not measured.** The chapter says so itself ("It does not show that the text generated for a page is grounded in that page", 04:519-521), and so does Chapter 7 ("its reliability end to end was not measured", 07:24).
  - **Only one of the four parts has an evaluation behind it:**

    | Part of the answer | Evidence |
    |---|---|
    | Route by the page | the routing study |
    | Decide before generating | the deck-plan scorecard, which pass (c) M7 calls circular |
    | Identity by content | none |
    | Write as you go | none after the change; the 703 s measurement is from before it |

  - **"Grounded" belongs to another question.** Chapter 2 defines it for *answers*: "We use the word *grounded* for an answer that is restricted to retrieved material" (02:97-99). That definition places groundedness in RQ3's territory, not in the pipeline's.
  - **So the RQ1 answer is a list of design properties.** It is not an answer to "how … reliably … grounded".
- **Proposed:**
  - (1) Reword RQ1 to what the chapter shows: "How can heterogeneous lecture PDFs be transformed into structured content that can be read at once, and which extraction methods give learners the most useful text at what cost?"
  - (2) Open the answer's first paragraph with: "Of the four parts below, the routing study evaluates the third, the scorecard confirms that the second is implemented, and the first and fourth are described, not measured."
  - (3) Use "grounded" only as Chapter 2 defines it.

### T2. Chapter 4's own yardstick is not closed

- **Where:** p. 20, `04-content.tex:70-72`; p. 30, `04-content.tex:493-517`.
- **Quoted:** "These four properties are the yardstick for the rest of the chapter: the pipeline is judged by how it handles a figure page, how soon a learner reads, what a deck costs, and what leaves the server."
- **What is wrong:** the Answer to RQ1 covers the four as follows:
  - **The figure page:** answered by routing.
  - **How soon a learner reads:** answered by write-as-you-go, but without a measurement after the change.
  - **What a deck costs:** never answered. The chapter reports cost per page for the extractors, and the caps (04:277-280). It gives no per-deck cost, although every parse writes a receipt with the cost of each call (Chapter 6, 06:446-451).
  - **What leaves the server:** never answered. The OpenAI-only mode, which exists to control exactly that, was not switched on (04:271-276). Production holds keys for six providers (03:129-132). Page images go to vision models.

  The chapter sets two tests and then does not report them.
- **Proposed:** add two sentences to the answer. The first: "A deck costs X in model calls (median over the parse receipts of the 25 decks)." That is one query on `parse_receipts`. The second: "What leaves the server is the page text, to the providers of the bulk chain, and the page image of 87 pages, to a vision model; the mode that would restrict this to the one provider the privacy policy names was not switched on at the pin." If the query cannot be run, cut the yardstick to the two properties the chapter answers.

### T3. RQ2 asks for "privacy-preserving" but no chapter defines the criterion, and the answer meets the weaker reading only

- **Where:**
  - p. 5, `01-introduction.tex:188-190` (RQ2)
  - p. 10, `02-background.tex:166-171` (k-anonymity)
  - p. 44, `05-loop.tex:548-551` (Answer)
  - p. 43, `05-loop.tex:510` (`\TODO` for the supervisor)
  - p. 55, `07-conclusion.tex:48-57`
- **Quoted:** "under k-anonymity a released record must be indistinguishable from at least k−1 others. Chapter 5 applies the idea not to released records but to released statements: nothing is said about a page that fewer than five people have read." And: "It can be fed back in a privacy-preserving way by limiting the output and not the storage".
- **What is wrong:**
  - **Two candidate criteria.** Chapter 2 sets up k-anonymity as the frame. The thesis then applies the floor to *readers of a page*, not to the people a statement is about.
  - **The frame is failed.** Chapter 5 shows that a hotspot can rest on one confused rater among five, and pass (b) N3 confirms this against the code. By Chapter 2's own frame, the view is not k-anonymous with k = 5.
  - **The question is never closed.** The Answer to RQ2 still says "privacy-preserving", and Chapter 7's RQ2 paragraph lists mechanisms and limits but never says whether the term holds. The `\TODO` defers that judgement to the supervisor.
  - **Why this is separate from the open item.** It overlaps the known open five-subject item, which asks whether the rule should change. The thread problem stands whichever rule is chosen: the RQ's key term has no criterion, so it cannot be answered yes or no.
- **Proposed:**
  - In §2.5 or §5.5, name the criterion. For example: "We call the view privacy-preserving if no statement it releases is about fewer than five people."
  - Then answer against it in §5.10 and Chapter 7: "By that criterion the view is not yet privacy-preserving: the floor counts the readers of a page, and one confused rater among five can carry a hotspot. It is privacy-preserving in the weaker sense that it names no one and says nothing about a page with fewer than five readers."
  - The supervisor's answer then decides only which sense the thesis claims.

### T4. RQ2's second clause asks what an owner learns; the answer gives what the view can show

- **Where:** p. 5, `01-introduction.tex:189-190`; p. 44, `05-loop.tex:557-563`.
- **Quoted:** RQ2: "… and what can an owner learn from them without seeing a single student?" Answer: "Without seeing a single student, an owner can learn which page confuses readers, where readers leave, …"
- **What is wrong:**

### T5. RQ3 asks about "learning content"; the measurements cover answer keys and one gate
