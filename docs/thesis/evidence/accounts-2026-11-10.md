# Accounts in production, counted 2026-11-10

*Source for the account sentence in section 1.4 of the thesis. Read-only queries against the production database (Supabase project `lkiiideqjoiksnycgplc`), each inside `BEGIN READ ONLY … ROLLBACK`. Only counts were read; no address is recorded here.*

## Why this replaces the earlier number

The thesis used to say "103 accounts that are not test accounts" for 30 October 2026. That number came from a filter on four address patterns and could not be reproduced on 10 November: the same filter gives 170. The count below uses a rule that anyone can re-run.

## The rule

An account counts as being **on a real mail domain** when its address ends in one of `gmail.com`, `hotmail.com`, `icloud.com` or a mail domain of the University of Marburg, and has no `+` in it. Every other account uses a test address, the reserved domain `example.com`, the project's demonstration domain, or an invented domain such as `a.com`.

"Active" means the account wrote at least one row to `public.learning_events` in the period.

## Queries

```sql
-- 1. all accounts, by domain
with u as (select split_part(lower(email),'@',2) d from auth.users)
select d, count(*) from u group by d order by 2 desc;

-- 2. accounts on real mail domains and their activity
with u as (select id, split_part(lower(email),'@',2) d, lower(email) e, created_at from auth.users),
r as (select * from u
      where d in ('gmail.com','hotmail.com','icloud.com' /* plus the University of Marburg's mail domains */)
        and e not like '%+%'),
a as (select user_id, max(created_at) last_ev from public.learning_events group by user_id)
select count(*),
       count(*) filter (where a.last_ev is not null),
       count(*) filter (where a.last_ev > now() - interval '90 days'),
       count(*) filter (where a.last_ev > now() - interval '30 days'),
       count(*) filter (where a.last_ev > now() - interval '7 days'),
       count(*) filter (where r.created_at >= '2026-09-01'),
       count(*) filter (where d like '%uni-marburg.de')
from r left join a on a.user_id = r.id;

-- 3. all accounts with an event in the last 7 and 30 days
select count(distinct user_id) from public.learning_events where created_at > now() - interval '7 days';
select count(distinct user_id) from public.learning_events where created_at > now() - interval '30 days';
```

## Counts on 2026-11-10

| | |
|---|---|
| All accounts | 216 |
| On `example.com` | 69 |
| On `test.com`, `admin.com` | 39 |
| On the demonstration domain | 15 |
| On other invented or project domains | 38 |
| **On a real mail domain** | **55** (gmail 46, hotmail 2, icloud 2, uni-marburg 5) |

Of the 55 accounts on a real mail domain:

| | |
|---|---|
| Ever active | 31 |
| Active in the last 90 days | 9 |
| Active in the last 30 days | 8 |
| Active in the last 7 days | 2 |
| Signed up since 1 September 2026 | 9 |

Across all 216 accounts, 2 were active in the last 7 days and 16 in the last 30.

## What the rule does not separate

The 55 include the developers' own accounts and test accounts with real-looking addresses. A further query on the developers' address prefixes (not recorded here) finds four such accounts among the 55, and one of the two accounts active in the last 7 days is one of them. A reading of the addresses by hand earlier the same day put 14 of the 55 down as people other than the developers; that reading is a judgement and cannot be re-run from this file, so the thesis does not use it.
