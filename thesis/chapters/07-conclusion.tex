\chapter{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:summary}

\paragraph{RQ1: from a PDF to readable pages.}
A learner reads the uploaded PDF at once, while a worker writes its
structured pages. A rule without a model call sends the pages without text
and the pages carried by a figure to a vision model: 87 of the 1{,}593 pages
of the corpus. In the blind benchmark the author preferred the transcription
by a vision model most, at under a cent a page, and ranked four of the ten
other methods above the production extraction in every analysis we ran
(\cref{sec:parser-bench,sec:c4-answer}). The author judged rendered outputs as a reader; we did not
measure what the models that later read the text do with them, nor whether
the generated text of a page is grounded in it.

\paragraph{RQ2: from learner events back to the owner.}
An engine turns a log of 17~checked event types into findings about pages,
which the owner's view shows without names and only for pages with at least
five readers. With scripted readers the view reported each of the five
behaviours that its rules look for, confusion, leaving, skipping, turning
back, and overconfidence, at all 100~seeds when an arm was built around it,
and nothing for a Space of four; five readers who asked about the same pages
raised no theme at any seed (\cref{sec:c5-answer}). The view falls short of
the stricter standard of \cref{sec:analytics-privacy}, that no statement is
about fewer than five people, because its floor counts the accounts that read
a page, which an owner can add, and not the people behind a finding
(\cref{sec:c5-privacy,sec:c5-findings}).

\paragraph{RQ3: checking the answer keys of generated quiz questions.}
Before learners see the deck quiz, a second model checks each of its
questions by answering it from the cited page without seeing the key. A
disputed question is rewritten once and dropped at a second dispute, and a
question whose check returns no verdict is kept, so that an outage does not
empty a quiz (\cref{sec:c6-lifecycle}). On 16~fixed pairs of a correct and a
wrong answer key, the similarity score failed its criterion fixed in advance,
and the check gave no wrong verdict in the 31 calls that returned one, which
still allows an error rate of up to about one in five
(\cref{sec:c6-results,sec:c6-threats}). That
result is in-sample, measured in the change that built the check, with no
criterion fixed in advance; the pairs' distractors were off the subject; and
we did not measure how often the generating model writes a wrong key.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The answers and the literature.}
On RQ1, the benchmark agrees with Dinh et al.\ that the page image carries
more than the text layer~\cite{dinh2025slides}, but on another task: they
measured a model's answers, and we measured a reader's preference. On RQ2,
the floor borrows the minimum group size of
$k$-anonymity~\cite{sweeney2002kanonymity}, but as a threshold on released
statistics, not as a property of released records (\cref{sec:c5-privacy}). On RQ3, the answer check runs on
another model than the writer, as the work on self-preference
advises~\cite{panickssery2024selfpreference}, though by default on a larger
model of the same family (\cref{sec:c6-lifecycle}).

\paragraph{Measure a method before relying on it.}
We chose the production extractor by uploading decks, looking at the titles,
page texts, and quizzes that came out, and fixing what failed
(\cref{sec:stack}). This made it work on the decks we tried, but its first
measured comparison was the benchmark of \cref{ch:content}, in which the
author preferred other methods to it. The similarity score of \cref{ch:trust}
went the other way: it also looked right, but we measured it before the system
relied on it, and it failed and stayed out of the system.

\paragraph{Write the expectation down first.}
To keep the code that coding agents wrote (\cref{sec:method-note}) in line
with our intention, we adopted practices that fix an expectation before the
work: a failing test first, a criterion fixed before a run, and a pinned
revision. The protocol of the benchmark was frozen before the first vote, and
each later change is a dated deviation (\cref{sec:parser-bench}). The sweep over 100~seeds followed a
protocol committed before it ran and showed that the published run of the
scripted readers had been a good draw (\cref{sec:c5-results}). We wrote the
23 expectations of that study before its first run but committed them with
its results, so their order rests on our word, not on a record.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Besides the limits of each chapter, seven hold for the thesis as a whole.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{No learners.} We ran no study with participants, and real use
    stayed too small to evaluate (\cref{sec:scope}). Nothing here shows that
    anyone learns more, or more willingly, with the system than with the PDF.
  \item \emph{The author is the judge.} The author, who built the system, made
    the benchmark votes and the routing labels and wrote the 16~pairs of the
    probe with AI assistance, without a second rater. The votes hid the method
    names, but later comparisons were less blind, and the pages and pairs were
    not selected independently.
  \item \emph{One university, small samples.} All material comes from a few
    born-digital courses of one university, in two languages. Twenty-four
    pages and sixteen pairs show where a method fails, not its rate on other
    material.
  \item \emph{No competing system.} The comparison in \cref{tab:related} rests
    on publications only.
  \item \emph{The author audited the author's system.} The security and
    privacy findings come from reading our own code and from a read-only probe
    of production (\cref{tab:deployed}). We ran no attack, and a second reader
    would find more.
  \item \emph{One machine.} We ran no load test and know nothing about the
    system under load.
  \item \emph{Two theses, one code base.} We draw the split in
    \cref{sec:scope} by commits. We discussed many design ideas together
    without writing them down, so the split of ideas is less sharp than the
    split of code.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:future-work}

\paragraph{A study with learners.}
The first next step is the missing study. One group reads the plain PDF and
one the reading view, both take a test right after reading and again days
later, and the event log of \cref{ch:loop} adds time on page and revisits.
Unlike the trial of Learn Your Way~\cite{learnlm2025textbook}, both groups
should get the same practice questions, so that the study isolates the
reading surface (\cref{sec:positioning}).

\paragraph{A fair test of the answer check.}
The answer check needs a test that it can fail: a sample of questions that
the model generated for new decks, with distractors on the subject of the
page, whose keys two raters label before the check runs, scored once against
a criterion fixed first.

\paragraph{The pipeline.}
The two results of \cref{ch:content} suggest a combination, the author's
idea, which we have not built or tested: pymu\-pdf4llm reads the text pages,
and the routing rule sends the pages without text and the pages carried by a
figure to a low-cost vision model. A new comparison should first judge
pymupdf4llm with its OCR pass off, as production would run it, against the
current extraction. Uploaders of the same file could share one parse once the
embeddings no longer move to whoever parsed last
(\code{unified\_orchestrator.py:785--791}).

\paragraph{The owner's view.}
The floor should count the people behind each finding
(\cref{sec:c5-findings}). The view also needs a defence against differencing
(\cref{sec:c5-privacy}); differential privacy would bound what one person
changes in the released numbers~\cite{dwork2006dp}.

\paragraph{One place to ask.}
Today a learner asks in three places, each with its own scope: Explain and
Ask on one page, Luna in a study session, and the ask bar across a Space
(\cref{ch:trust}). One place to ask could choose the scope from the question,
the open page, its neighbours, or the whole Space, and apply that scope's
checks. This too is the author's idea, not built or tested.

\paragraph{Closing.}
\label{sec:reflection}
The habit the project left us with is to write the expectation down, to
measure, and to report the result when it disappoints.
