This appendix holds nine diagrams that the body refers to.

\cref{fig:use-case,fig:domain-model} to \cref{sec:stakeholders},
\cref{fig:er,fig:er-learning} to \cref{sec:data-model},

\thesisfigurepage{f8-pipeline}
  {The ingestion pipeline. Two entry routes admit a PDF synchronously and
   converge on one content-addressed object and one Arq job, which writes each
   page as soon as it is synthesised. Pentagons mark where the reader gains
   something --- the original PDF at once, then each persisted page.}
  {fig:pipeline}

\thesisfigure{f4-use-case}
  {Actors and goals as implemented. Student goals are served by the current
   interface. Whether a user may create and publish a Space depends on
   ownership, not on the account type, and professor and admin goals remain in
   the older interface. The shaded goal is built but
   switched off in production.}
  {fig:use-case}

\thesisfigure{f5-domain-model}
  {Conceptual domain model. Analysis-level vocabulary, deliberately distinct
   from the physical database schema.}
  {fig:domain-model}

\thesisfigurepage{f9-er-core}
  {Core physical schema, part 1: identity, Spaces, content, and parsing --- 19
   of the 36 core tables, out of 79 tables defined in the migrations
   (distinct \code{CREATE TABLE} names, one of them the default partition of
   the event table). The dashed line marks a reference without a database-enforced foreign
   key.}
  {fig:er}

\thesisfigurepage{f9b-er-learning}
  {Core physical schema, part 2: the 17 learner-facing tables (practice, Luna
   sessions, notes, gamification), attached to grey stubs of part-1 tables.
   Dashed lines have no database-enforced foreign key, and two are polymorphic
   \code{space\_id} text links.}
  {fig:er-learning}

\thesisfigure{f15-security-arc}
  {Privilege-escalation hardening in two phases: five steps over six
   migrations from January to June 2026, then four steps over five migrations
   from October to November 2026. Steps 1 and 3 each opened a hole that a
   later step closed, and no step marks the hardening as complete.}
  {fig:security-arc}

\thesisfigure{f14-trust-boundaries}
  {Trust boundaries. A browser holding the anonymous key meets three
   database-side boundaries --- table row-level security, function execute grants, and storage policies. Most backend routes bypass row-level security
   and check ownership in application code.}
  {fig:trust-boundaries}

\thesisfigurepage{f12-tutor-sequence}
  {The grounded course tutor behind the home ask bar. Groundedness is enforced
   at five points, including a refusal before any model call.}
  {fig:tutor-sequence}

\thesisfigure{f16-citation-gap}
  {Source attribution. The server's validated citations reach learners only through the
   ask bar of the current interface, while the older interface still discards them. At
   \thesisEarlierCommit{}, no screen a student could reach showed a source.}
  {fig:citation-gap}
